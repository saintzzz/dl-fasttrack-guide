Where VJP wins

  • True VVIP product - $300 buys non-stop escort through everything; most competitors cap out lower or fuzz the top tier.
  • Granular pricing - published table with airport-specific deltas (SGN surcharges) instead of a single "from" number.
  • Four airports - SGN, HAN, DAD, PQC including Phu Quoc.

Where it loses ground

  • No instant checkout - booking runs through contact form; the beautiful price table is informational, not shoppable directly.
  • Airline restrictions - departure Priority not available for VietJet at SGN/DAD, and Premium is business-class-only at SGN - check your ticket first.
  • "Base rate" caveat - night/early-morning and special requests may add charges beyond the table.

The verdict

VJP is the pick when you want the ceiling product - non-stop VVIP at $300, or need Phu Quoc. At the practical $35-50 end it trades within a few dollars of rivals; there the decider is booking flow, and form-based quote loops lose to instant checkout when time is short. Frequently asked questions

Is VJP legit?

Yes - published tier pricing across 4 airports, one of the most detailed public price structures available.

How much?

Arrival $35-300, departure $50-300 per person; SGN carries small surcharges on some tiers.

Which airports?

SGN, HAN, DAD, PQC - no Cam Ranh.

VJP vs AAL?

VJP: higher ceiling (VVIP $300), PQC coverage, form booking. AAL: instant checkout + 2h confirm, VIP B no-stop tier, no PQC.

Is VJP the same as Vietnam Fast Track (vietnam-fasttrack.com)?

They share branding DNA and both route you to a sales chat rather than a checkout - treat them as quote-based concierge operators. Always get the final price, what is included, and the cancellation terms in writing inside the chat before paying.

Why do VJP pages rank so high on Google/Bing?

Aggressive SEO on an exact-match domain plus heavy content volume. Ranking position says nothing about service quality - judge them like any provider: written price, named agent, confirmed meeting point, and what happens if they no-show.
