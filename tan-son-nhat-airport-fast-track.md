## Why the Ho Chi Minh City airport queue is the worst in Vietnam

Three problems compound at Terminal 2:

- Volume over capacity. The international terminal handles far more passengers than it was designed for, and counter staffing lags the schedule.

- The evening wave. Most long-haul flights from Europe, Australia, the Middle East and Northeast Asia land between 6pm and 11pm - several wide-bodies at once, all funneled into the same hall.

- The visa-on-arrival desk. Travelers on approval letters stop at the Landing Visa counter first - a second queue inside the same hall that can add 20-40 minutes on its own.

Even off-peak, Ho Chi Minh City's airport is rarely fast: 30-45 minutes is a good midday result. During Tet and the June-August peak, waits beyond 100 minutes are common. Travelers also know it as Saigon airport - same terminals, same queues.

## The arrival flow, step by step

- Gate to immigration hall - a walk down to the ground floor of T2; queues often start on the escalators.

- Landing Visa counter (VoA letters only) - exchange the approval letter for the visa sticker before joining the main queue.

- Immigration counters - the main queue: passport, visa check, photo. 30-100+ minutes.

- Baggage claim - belts are one level down; bags are usually waiting after a long queue.

- Customs + arrivals hall - green channel walk-through. SIM counters, currency exchange, and the taxi/Grab lanes are outside.

## How fast track works at SGN

- The agent meets you at the arrival gate or aerobridge exit of T2 with a name board.

- On a visa-on-arrival letter? The agent handles the Landing Visa desk paperwork while you wait.

- You are escorted through the priority immigration lane - a few minutes at the counter instead of an hour in the hall.

- Escort continues to baggage claim and out to the arrivals hall. Total: about 10-15 minutes gate to curb.

## When it pays off at SGN

- Any 6-11pm arrival - this is the default worst window, not an edge case.

- Weekends and holidays - Tet especially; the hall is at its worst when the country is traveling.

- Tight domestic connections - domestic flights now use T1 and the newer T3, a 5-15 minute landside transfer. An hour in the immigration queue can decide whether you make it.

- Families and elderly travelers - standing an hour-plus after a long-haul flight is rough on kids and grandparents.

Skip-it case: a quiet midday midweek arrival in low season. Even then, expect ~30 minutes, not zero.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long is the immigration queue at Tan Son Nhat?** — 60-100+ minutes at peak: evenings 6-11pm, weekends, Tet and public holidays. Off-peak midday can be 30-45 minutes.

**Which terminal does fast track use at Tan Son Nhat?** — Terminal 2 - the international terminal. Domestic flights use Terminal 1 and the newer Terminal 3; fast track applies to international arrivals at T2.

**Where does the agent meet you at SGN?** — At the arrival gate or aerobridge exit of Terminal 2 with a name board. The agent handles visa-on-arrival paperwork if needed and escorts you through the priority lane.

**Is fast track worth it at Tan Son Nhat?** — More than at any other Vietnam airport. Landing 6-11pm, on a weekend or around Tet, the regular queue regularly exceeds an hour - fast track cuts it to 10-15 minutes.

**Does it help with a tight domestic connection?** — Yes - domestic departures use T1 or T3, a 5-15 minute transfer landside. Skipping a 60-100 minute queue is often the difference between making and missing the connection.
