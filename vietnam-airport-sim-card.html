Vietnam Airport SIM Card: Prices, Counters and Options

You need data the moment you land - for Grab, for maps, for messaging your hotel. There are three real ways to get connected at Vietnamese airports, and the right one depends on whether your phone takes eSIM.

Option 1: eSIM before you fly (smoothest)

If your phone supports eSIM - iPhone XS and later, Pixel 3+, recent Samsung Galaxy - buy a travel eSIM at home, scan the QR on Wi-Fi, and your phone connects the moment you land. No queue, no passport photocopy, no physical swap. Downside: most travel eSIMs are data-only, so no Vietnamese phone number for local calls (rarely needed - Grab and messaging run on data).

Option 2: Pre-ordered SIM with airport pickup

You book a specific data package online before the trip, get a pickup code, and collect the SIM at a named counter in the arrivals hall. Same price range as a walk-up purchase but you skip the "which plan do I want" negotiation at the counter after a long flight - and you know the package is real, not a marked-up tourist bundle. This is the middle path most travelers should take when they want a physical SIM with a local number.

Option 3: Walk-up airport counter

SIM counters sit in the arrivals hall just past customs at all three major airports - you cannot miss them. Staff usually speak basic English, plans are posted on boards, and they install the SIM for you. Two catches: the queue after a big flight bank can take 15-20 minutes, and counter packages run a few dollars above city prices for the same data.

Which carrier?

  • Viettel - widest coverage, best choice for Sapa, Ha Giang, islands and rural routes.
  • Vinaphone - strong in cities and the coastal corridor.
  • Mobifone - solid in the big three cities, thinner deep in the countryside.

For a Hanoi / HCMC / Da Nang / Hoi An itinerary any of the three is fine. Heading into the northern mountains or remote islands - take Viettel.

Practical notes

  • Passport required. Vietnamese law requires SIM registration - the counter will photograph or photocopy your passport.
  • Data sizes are generous. Tourist packages typically offer 5-15GB/day or ~60-120GB/month - streaming-level amounts. You will not run out.
  • Grab needs data. The cheapest airport transfer is a Grab car or bike; having data before you walk out of the terminal is genuinely useful, not a luxury.
  • Keep your home SIM safe. If you swap SIMs, put the old one in the packet the new SIM came in - they vanish easily.

Frequently asked questions

Should I buy a SIM at the airport or before I fly?

eSIM before departure is smoothest if your phone supports it. For a physical SIM with a local number, pre-ordering for airport pickup beats queuing at the counter after a long flight.

How much is a SIM at Vietnam airports?

Walk-up tourist SIMs run about US$8-15 for 7-30 days of data. Pre-ordered pickup SIMs and eSIMs are the same range or cheaper; city shops are cheapest but need a trip after arrival.

Which carrier has the best coverage?

Viettel is widest, especially rural and mountain areas. Vinaphone and Mobifone are fine for city-and-coast itineraries.

Can I get a Vietnam eSIM?

Yes - travel eSIMs and Vietnamese carriers both offer QR-activated eSIMs. Buy before flying, install on Wi-Fi, land connected.
