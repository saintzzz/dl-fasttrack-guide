## Three ways people arrive - and what each needs

| Entry type | What you bring | Extra stop |

| e-Visa | Printed e-visa PDF + passport | None - straight to immigration |

| Visa on arrival (VoA) | Approval letter + NA1 form + 2 photos + stamping fee (USD cash) | Landing Visa desk before immigration |

| Visa-free passport | Passport only (eligible countries) | None |

## Before you fly - papers that matter

- e-Visa holders - print the PDF. Phone copies usually work but paper is safer: no screen glare, no dead battery, no officer who insists on a printed page. Keep it in hand luggage, not checked bags.

- VoA travelers - the printed approval letter is mandatory, plus a completed NA1 form (fill it on the plane if you can) and passport photos. Bring the stamping fee in clean USD notes - cards are not accepted at the desk.

- Visa-free passports (Japan, Korea, most of Europe, ASEAN) - just the passport. Check your permitted stay length; officers occasionally ask for an onward ticket.

## Step by step at the airport

- Disembark and walk to the immigration hall - 3-10 minutes depending on gate.

- e-Visa and visa-free travelers go straight to immigration counters.

- VoA travelers stop at the Landing Visa counter first - submit letter, NA1, photos, pay the stamping fee, get the stamp. That desk has its own queue, then you join the immigration queue.

- Immigration counters - passport check, photo taken, entry stamp. The officer may ask for your onward ticket or hotel - answer briefly.

- Baggage claim - your bags are usually already on the belt by the time you get through.

- Customs - green channel for nothing to declare. Random bag scans happen.

## Where the time goes

On a VoA letter the visa counter can add 20-40 minutes on its own at peak. Immigration then adds 20-90 minutes depending on the airport and time of day - see our immigration wait times guide for per-airport numbers. A fast track agent meets you at the gate, walks you through the visa paperwork if needed, and escorts you through the priority immigration lane - 10-15 minutes gate to exit is normal.

## Mistakes that get travelers pulled aside

- Wrong port of entry on the e-visa - e-visas are tied to the entry point you selected. Landing at a different airport than the one listed can mean re-applying or a long conversation. Check the entry point code on your PDF before departure.

- Passport valid under 6 months - airlines can deny boarding, and Vietnam will not let you in.

- Name order mismatched - the e-visa must match the passport exactly, including middle names. Common issue for travelers who abbreviate.

- VoA letter for the wrong date or airport - approval letters are date- and airport-specific.

- No USD cash for the stamping fee - the VoA desk does not take cards and the ATM is landside.

- Overstaying visa-free days - the day count starts on arrival day. Officers do check stamps for frequent visitors.

## Combining e-visa with fast track

With an e-visa the flow is simplest - the agent just takes you straight to the priority lane. With a VoA letter the agent handles the visa desk for you, which is where most of the confusion and waiting happens. Either way, booking at least 12 hours ahead is required so staff can meet your flight, and the agent tracks your flight number so normal delays are covered.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I use fast track with a Vietnam e-visa?** — Yes - e-visa is the simplest combination. The fast track agent meets you at the gate and takes you straight to the priority immigration lane. You skip only the queue, not the visa check itself.

**Do I need to print my Vietnam e-visa?** — Printing the PDF is strongly recommended. Phone copies usually work, but paper avoids issues with screen glare, dead batteries and officers who insist on a printed page. Keep it in your hand luggage, not checked bags.

**What is the difference between e-visa and visa on arrival in Vietnam?** — E-visa holders go straight to immigration counters - no extra stop. Visa-on-arrival requires a printed approval letter, NA1 form and passport photos, plus a stop at the Landing Visa desk for the stamp before immigration, which adds 20-40 minutes at peak.

**How early should I book fast track for Vietnam?** — Book at least 12 hours before your flight so the operator can assign an agent to meet your arrival. Same-day bookings are sometimes possible but not guaranteed, especially during Tet and peak season.

**What if my flight is delayed - will the fast track agent wait?** — Agents track your flight number and adjust for delays. If you miss the connection entirely or change flights, contact the hotline to reschedule - do not just show up at a different time.

**Is fast track the same as visa on arrival?** — No. Fast track is a meet-and-greet service that skips the immigration queue - it is not a visa. You still need a valid e-visa, visa-free passport, or VoA approval letter. Fast track can also escort VoA passengers through the visa desk.
